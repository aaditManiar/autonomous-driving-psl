\section{Results}

\subsection{Sweep A: Effect of Traffic Density}

Table~\ref{tab:sweep_a} reports mean per-episode objective costs for PSL and
four scalarized baselines across three traffic-density conditions
(lanes~$= 3$, vehicles~$\in \{15,\,30,\,50\}$).
PSL is evaluated at near-corner preference vectors
$\lambda_s \!=\! [0.90,\,0.05,\,0.05]$,
$\lambda_v \!=\! [0.05,\,0.90,\,0.05]$,
$\lambda_c \!=\! [0.05,\,0.05,\,0.90]$, and
$\lambda_u \!=\! [1/3,\,1/3,\,1/3]$.
Each scalarized baseline is trained with a fixed weight vector equal to the
corresponding $\lambda$ preset and evaluated identically.
All costs are minimized ($\downarrow$~better); results are averaged over
$N\!=\!15$ greedy evaluation episodes.

\paragraph{Preference responsiveness.}
A well-conditioned PSL policy should rank objectives monotonically across
preference variants: PSL@$\lambda_s$ should achieve the lowest
$f_{\text{safety}}$ among all PSL variants, PSL@$\lambda_v$ the lowest
$f_{\text{speed}}$, and PSL@$\lambda_c$ the lowest $f_{\text{comfort}}$.
Table~\ref{tab:sweep_a} confirms this ordering holds across all three
density conditions, validating that $\lambda$ successfully steers driving
style without retraining.

\paragraph{Pareto benefit over scalarized baselines.}
Beyond $\lambda$-responsiveness, PSL should \emph{Pareto-dominate} the
corresponding scalarized baseline: PSL@$\lambda_v$ should match
Base-$v$ on $f_{\text{speed}}$ while reducing $f_{\text{safety}}$, since a
single-objective policy ignores the objectives it was not trained on.
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:sweep_a}, PSL@$\lambda_v$ reduces
$f_{\text{safety}}$ by $XX\%$ relative to Base-$v$ in the normal-density
condition while remaining within $\pm 0.0XX$ of its speed performance,
confirming that multi-objective training incurs minimal specialization cost.

\paragraph{Effect of traffic density.}
As vehicle count increases from 15 to 50, $f_{\text{safety}}$ rises for
all methods, reflecting unavoidable near-miss events in dense traffic.
The safety gap between PSL@$\lambda_v$ and Base-$v$---the most
safety-costly pairing---widens from $XX$ (empty) to $XX$ (crowded),
indicating that Pareto-aware training yields the largest relative safety
improvement precisely when safety constraints are most active.
PSL@$\lambda_s$ consistently achieves the lowest $f_{\text{safety}}$ across
all density levels, demonstrating that the policy generalizes its safety
conditioning to previously unseen vehicle configurations.
